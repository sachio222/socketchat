Run `socketchat` to open the chat screen. It asks for your passphrase, then shows your rooms. It works in any terminal window of 80 by 24 characters or larger, and over SSH.

<Note>This picture shows the phosphor colors, the default. `/theme classic` switches to your terminal's own colors; see [Colors](#colors).</Note>

## Panels

| Panel | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Chats** | Your rooms, marked `#`, and direct messages, marked `@`. A number shows new messages. |
| **Messages** | The open room, with times and names. The panel title is the room's name and its server, as in `Book club · chat.example.com`. When the room has a topic, the first line under the title shows it, as in `Topic: Piranesi, chapters 1 to 5`, cut to one line; `/details` shows all of it. Each room shows once that it is end-to-end encrypted; see [The encryption line](/security/encryption#what-the-chat-screen-shows). |
| **People** | Everyone in the room. `✓` marks someone you verified, and `*` marks an admin. The title counts the verified marks, yours included, as in "People 2 of 3 ✓", shortened to "People 2/3 ✓" or "2/3 ✓" in a narrow window. It reads "People ! check", and a `!` marks the row, while someone shows under the name of a person you verified with a different key; see [Someone new under a verified name](/use/rooms-and-invites#someone-new-under-a-verified-name). |
| **Message box** | Where you type messages and commands. The empty box reads "What's on your mind?" with the open room's name. |

The bottom line shows the keys that work right now, and the Socketchat version when the window is wide enough. When a newer release is out, it says so, as in "Socketchat 0.6.1 is out. Run socketchat update."; see [Updating](/help/updating#the-update-notice).

## The room's server

Each room lives on one server, and the Messages panel title names it after the room's name. The server shows as its host, with the port added when it is not 7444. In a narrow window the title drops the server and keeps the room's name. When the app has lost its connection, the title ends in "offline".

New rooms from `/new` go on the open room's server, or on the newest server you joined when no room is open. `/servers` lists every server you use and whether each is connected, and `/details` names the open room's server. See [Servers](/use/commands#servers).

## Keys

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| Enter | Send the message, or run the command. When only one command starts with what you typed, run it, so `/q` quits, or put it in the box to type the rest, so `/jo` gives `/join `. A lone `/` opens the list of commands. |
| Tab, Shift-Tab | Move between panels. While you type a command, complete it instead, and after `/switch`, `/invite` or `/join` put in a room or a name. |
| Ctrl-K | Open the room switcher. Type to filter, Enter to open. |
| Up, Down | In the message box, after `/` and a letter, step through the commands that start with those letters, and after `/switch`, `/invite` or `/join` step through the rooms or names Tab would give. Otherwise bring back your earlier lines in this room. Elsewhere, pick a room in Chats or a person in People, or scroll the messages. |
| PgUp, PgDn | Scroll the messages. |
| Esc | Close a box, or clear a selection. |
| Y, N | Answer a question, such as whether to send a video. Only Y as the first key pressed means yes. |
| Ctrl-C | Quit when pressed twice within 2 seconds. After the first press the bottom line says "Press Ctrl-C again to quit.", and any other key, or 2 seconds passing, starts over. With text selected, Ctrl-C copies it instead. `/quit` quits at once. |

## Find a command

Type `/` and Enter, or `/help`, to open a list of every command in alphabetical order, with the first one highlighted.

- Up and Down move the highlight and go round from the last command to the first. The mouse wheel moves it too, and PgUp and PgDn scroll a page.
- Enter runs the highlighted command, or puts it in the message box with a space after it when it needs more, such as `/join`.
- A click on a command highlights it, and a second click uses it.
- Esc closes the list.

To step through commands without the list, type `/` and a letter: Up and Down put each command that starts with those letters in the message box in turn, in alphabetical order, going round. Typing ends the run. With a lone `/`, an empty box or a message, Up and Down bring back earlier lines instead.

Tab completes a command as you type it, and Tab or Shift-Tab steps through the matches, in alphabetical order, when there are several. Matching commands show on the bottom line, and a grey suggestion after the cursor shows what Tab would add; Right arrow accepts it. `/theme` and Tab cycle the color names.

## Complete rooms and names

After `/switch`, `/invite` or `/join` and a space, Tab puts in a room or a name, and each further Tab moves to the next, going round. Shift-Tab goes back. Letters after the space narrow the list: `/join Cl` and Tab takes only the rooms that start with "Cl".

| After | Tab puts in |
|---|---|
| `/switch` | One of your rooms. Enter opens the room shown. |
| `/invite` | The name of someone who shares a room with you, with people who are not in the open room first. Two people with the same name come up as `Bo#1a2b` and `Bo#9f3c`, so the command names one person, and a name with a space comes up in quotes. Someone whose name has not reached your app yet does not come up, since `/invite` cannot name them either. |
| `/join` | The room of an invite that waits for you, newest first. With no invite waiting, the bottom line says "No invite waits for you." |

Up and Down step through the same list: Down puts in the next room or name that Tab would, and Up the one before, going round. `/help` lists these keys.

See [Commands](/use/commands) for the full list.

## Earlier lines

In the message box, Up brings back the last message you sent or command you ran in the open room, and each further Up goes one line further back. Down goes forward again and, past the newest line, gives back what you were typing. While an earlier line shows, the bottom line says what Up and Down do, and `/help` lists them.

- Each room keeps its own lines, the newest 100.
- A line repeated in a row is kept once.
- Socketchat keeps them in memory only and never writes them to disk, so they go when Socketchat closes.
- `/leave`, `/clear` and `/forget` drop a room's lines at once.

## Pasting

A paste goes into the message box as text, with line breaks shown as `↵`. Nothing is sent or run until you press Enter, so a pasted command never runs by itself. This needs a terminal that marks pastes (bracketed paste), which most do.

## Drop a file

Drag a photo, video or other file onto the window, and the message box shows `[Photo 1]`, `[Video 1]` or `[File 1]` while the bottom line names it, as in "Photo 1 is beach.jpg, 2.1 MB. Enter sends it." Enter sends each file as `/send` does, then any words you typed as a message, and Backspace removes a placeholder with its file. See [Drop a file on the window](/use/files-photos-videos#drop-a-file-on-the-window).

## Mouse

The mouse is on by default:

- Click a room in Chats to open it, and click a panel to move to it.
- Scroll the messages, `/help`, `/invites`, `/details` and `/status` with the wheel.
- Boxes have buttons along their bottom edge, such as Yes and No. A click answers only once the box is on screen.

### Select and copy

Drag over messages to select them, and Socketchat copies the text when you let go. The bottom line says "Copied.", or that Socketchat asked your terminal to copy when no clipboard program could.

- A message the screen wrapped copies as one line, and a message of several lines copies its lines without the indent the screen puts under the first.
- Dragging past the top or bottom of the messages scrolls them a row at a time.
- Text in the `/help`, `/details`, `/about`, `/status`, `/invites` and `/servers` boxes copies the same way.
- Esc, typing, a new click or new messages clear the selection.
- Ctrl-C while text is selected copies it, as letting go of the drag does.

To select the terminal's way instead, hold Shift as you drag (Option or Fn on a Mac). To hand the mouse back to your terminal, type:

```text
/mouse off
```

`/mouse on` turns it back on, and `/mouse` says which it is. Socketchat keeps the choice in your profile.

## Colors

Socketchat has two color themes:

- **phosphor**, the default: green on a near-black screen.
- **classic**: your terminal's own background and colors.

```text
/theme classic
/theme phosphor
```

`/theme` on its own shows the colors in use. Socketchat saves the choice on your computer. To pick a theme for one run only, set `SOCKETCHAT_THEME`:

```bash
SOCKETCHAT_THEME=classic socketchat
```

Phosphor uses exact colors when your terminal reports full color support (`COLORTERM` set to `truecolor` or `24bit`), and close 256-color matches otherwise.
